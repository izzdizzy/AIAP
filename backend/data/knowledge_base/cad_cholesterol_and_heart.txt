Cholesterol and Heart Disease Knowledge Base

1. What Is Cholesterol:
- Waxy, fat-like substance found in cell walls. Used for hormone production, vitamin D synthesis, and bile acids.
- Produced mainly by the liver, supplemented by dietary animal fats.

2. Types of Cholesterol:
- LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein): "Bad" cholesterol. Deposits excess cholesterol in arterial walls, forming atherosclerotic plaque.
- HDL (High-Density Lipoprotein): "Good" cholesterol. Transports cholesterol away from arteries back to the liver for excretion.
- Triglycerides: Type of fat in blood. High levels combined with high LDL increase coronary artery disease (CAD) risk.

3. Risk Factors & Atherosclerosis:
- Excess LDL penetrates blood vessel walls, causing inflammation and arterial narrowing (atherosclerosis).
- Plaque buildup restricts oxygenated blood supply to heart muscle, leading to angina (chest pain) or myocardial infarction (heart attack).
- High blood pressure (hypertension) damages vessel linings, accelerating plaque deposition.

4. Actionable Management Targets:
- Desirable Total Cholesterol: < 200 mg/dL (< 5.2 mmol/L).
- Desirable LDL Cholesterol: < 100 mg/dL (< 2.6 mmol/L) for moderate/high risk patients.
- Desirable HDL Cholesterol: > 40 mg/dL for men, > 50 mg/dL for women.
- Triglycerides: < 150 mg/dL (< 1.7 mmol/L).
